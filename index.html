Weizhen Wang

Ph.D. student · Shanghai Jiao Tong University

I am a Ph.D. student in the School of Automation and Intelligent Sensing at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, advised by Prof. Xiaoming Duan. I received my B.E. from Zhejiang University in 2023.

My research explores sequential decision-making and learning in robotic systems, with persistent monitoring as a central application. I am interested in how robot teams can plan and coordinate efficiently over time. My work ranges from studying stochastic patrols through Markov-chain models to developing multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approaches for deterministic monitoring, using Markov decision process formulations.

Building on this work, I study broader theoretical and algorithmic questions in reinforcement learning (RL) and MARL, including the theoretical analysis of policy-gradient methods and the development of efficient MARL algorithms for robotic patrolling.

I am currently exploring new research directions at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning. Please feel free to contact me to exchange research ideas or explore opportunities for collaboration!

Maximizing Markov Trajectory Entropy under Kemeny Constraints for Robotic Surveillance

Xiaoming Duan, Weizhen Wang, Rui Yan

IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, 71(7): 4850–4857, 2026.

We design unpredictable robotic patrols by maximizing Markov trajectory entropy while constraining coverage efficiency through Kemeny’s constant. A closed-form identity links trajectory entropy to the product of entropy rate and Kemeny’s constant, motivating an explicit efficiency bound and enabling analysis of the resulting optimal chains. A numerical surveillance example illustrates the effectiveness of the resulting strategy against intruders that wait before launching an attack.

Minimizing Worst-Case Weighted Latency for Multi-Robot Persistent Monitoring: Theory and RL-Based Solutions

Weizhen Wang, Ziheng Wang, Jianping He, Xinping Guan, Xiaoming Duan

arXiv:2605.09633 · 2026. Preprint.

How should a robot team revisit important locations without leaving any unattended for too long? We introduce tail-performance objectives for persistent monitoring on weighted graphs, establish their theoretical properties, and reformulate the problem as an event-driven Markov decision process. This enables reinforcement-learning solutions and M2Bench, a shared benchmark for comparing learned and heuristic monitoring strategies across synthetic and realistic environments.

Joint Optimization of Defense Allocations and Surveillance Strategies against Random Intruders

Weizhen Wang, Xiaoming Duan

IFAC-PapersOnLine · 2026. Accepted.

This work jointly designs defense-resource allocations and Markov-chain patrol strategies to increase the probability of capturing random intruders. An alternating optimization method updates the patrol strategy and resource allocation in turn. We derive gradients for patrol optimization, show that a greedy method optimally allocates defenses for a fixed strategy, and relate capture probability to Kemeny’s constant as a surveillance-design criterion.

Analysis of On-policy Policy Gradient Methods under the Distribution Mismatch

Weizhen Wang, Xinzhe Zhou, Jianping He, Xiaoming Duan

arXiv:2503.22244 · 2025; updated October 2026.

Conference version accepted to the IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC). Journal version submitted.

Why do practical policy-gradient algorithms work even when their sampling distribution differs from the one required by theory? We analyze this mismatch, establish global convergence for tabular policies, and characterize when it speeds up or slows down asymptotic convergence. For more general policy parameterizations, explicit mismatch bounds clarify how the gap depends on the discount factor and connect theoretical guarantees with practical implementations.

Growing Well-Connected Networks for Entropy Rate Maximization

Weizhen Wang, Xiaoming Duan, Jianping He

IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, 70(11): 7636–7643, 2025.

Which new connections make a network support more unpredictable Markov-chain motion? This work studies how to select additional edges to increase the maximum achievable entropy rate. By treating edge additions as perturbations, it derives a linear approximation of their effect and builds efficient edge-selection heuristics. The analysis links these updates to directions of greatest improvement, while simulations assess their effectiveness across growing networks.

Multirobot Stochastic Patrolling via Graph Partitioning

Weizhen Wang, Xiaoming Duan, Jianping He

IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems, 12(1): 300–312, 2025.

How should a team of robots divide an environment for efficient randomized patrols? This work assigns each robot a subgraph and a Markov-chain patrol strategy, using a team performance metric based on first hitting times. For unweighted complete graphs, the partitioning problem connects to maximum k-cut. A structure-aware descent heuristic offers finite-step convergence and a suboptimality bound, with numerical experiments evaluating the resulting patrol strategies.

On the Trade-Off Between Efficiency and Unpredictability in Stochastic Robotic Surveillance

Weizhen Wang, Jianping He, Xiaoming Duan

IEEE Control Systems Letters, 8: 2829–2834, 2024.

Fast patrols and unpredictable motion can pull a surveillance strategy in different directions. This paper studies that tension through Kemeny's constant and entropy rate, characterizing their Pareto trade-off for Markov-chain patrols. It then compares efficient strategies using distance to an ideal point and mixing rate, developing a convex optimization formulation for the former and a bisection-based heuristic for the latter.
